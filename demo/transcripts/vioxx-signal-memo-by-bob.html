Rofecoxib (Vioxx) — Disproportionality Signal Report

Drug: ROFECOXIB / VIOXX  ·  Total FAERS database: 20,692,690 reports  ·  Reports mentioning drug: 45,770  ·  Criteria: Evans (2001) PRR ≥ 2, χ² ≥ 4, n ≥ 3
Bottom Line Up Front

Rofecoxib carries extremely large, statistically unambiguous cardiovascular disproportionality signals in FAERS — dominated by myocardial infarction (PRR 49, n = 19,886), coronary artery disease (PRR 72, n = 4,157), and cerebrovascular accident (PRR 39, n = 13,371) — findings quantitatively consistent with the clinical evidence that led to voluntary market withdrawal in September 2004; these signals cannot be attributed to random variation, though litigation-driven reporting severely inflates case counts and must be treated as a major confound before any incidence estimate is derived.

5 · Likely Reporting Biases

  • Litigation-driven notoriety bias (primary confound). Rofecoxib was withdrawn in September 2004 and became the subject of the largest pharmaceutical litigation in US history. Reports received years after withdrawal — visible in the 2014 receive dates in the case sample — are overwhelmingly legal-process submissions rather than spontaneous clinical observations. This artificially inflates n and chi² without adding independent signal evidence.
  • Channelling / confounding by indication. Rofecoxib was preferentially prescribed to patients with osteoarthritis and chronic pain who frequently had underlying cardiovascular risk factors (hypertension, dyslipidaemia, diabetes). Cardiovascular events in this population may partly reflect background disease burden rather than drug effect.
  • Co-reporter clustering. The same report (#10044867) appears in MI, CVA, and CAD queries simultaneously. A single complex case inflates counts across multiple reaction terms and should not be treated as independent evidence for each.
  • Masking by other drugs. Rosiglitazone (Avandia) appears as a co-drug in several sampled reports. Its own cardiovascular controversy generates reports in the same MI / CAD space, potentially inflating the background (c cell) for rofecoxib and, paradoxically, could understate true PRRs if both drugs appear together; the direction is case-mix dependent.
  • Selective under-reporting of non-events. FAERS captures adverse events, not drug exposures without events; denominator data are unavailable. PRR measures reporting disproportionality, not event rate.

6 · Recommended Next Steps

  1. Run signal_emergence_timeline for MI and CVA with exclude_drugs="rosiglitazone, avandia" to correct for masking by the co-reported rosiglitazone signal and establish when the rofecoxib cardiovascular signal would first have met detection criteria in a prospective FAERS screen.
  2. Stratify by receive-date cohort. Separate pre-withdrawal (≤ 2004) from post-withdrawal reports to isolate litigation-era submissions and assess whether the signal was detectable from real-time reporting before the VIGOR trial results were public.
  3. Apply Bayesian metrics (BCPNN / EBGM) as a cross-check; PRR in highly imbalanced tables (n = 19 k vs background n = 182 k) can be numerically unstable; Bayesian shrinkage provides a complementary, more conservative signal estimate.
  4. Link to structured clinical evidence. Cross-reference signal reactions against the VIGOR (2000) and APPROVe (2004) RCT results and the FDA CDER safety review (2004) to verify pharmacological plausibility and regulatory timeline consistency.
  5. De-duplicate multi-reaction reports before presenting any case-count figure publicly; the current n values reflect event–report pairs, not unique patients.
Causation disclaimer: All statistics in this memo measure disproportionality of reporting in a spontaneous adverse-event database. A high PRR indicates that events are reported more often alongside this drug than alongside other drugs in the same database. It does not establish that the drug caused the event, does not estimate population-level incidence, and must not be used as a standalone basis for regulatory action without further analysis.
