## Rosiglitazone × Myocardial Infarction — Signal Detection Analysis

### 1. The Masking Problem: Vioxx in the Background

Before looking at numbers, the mechanism must be understood. PRR compares the proportion of MI reports among rosiglitazone reports against the proportion of MI reports in *all other FAERS reports* (the background). If another drug floods that background with its own MI reports, the background proportion inflates — and rosiglitazone's relative signal gets diluted toward 1.0 (or even below it), making it look safe.

Vioxx's own cardiovascular liability, combined with a wave of personal-injury litigation that generated massive medico-legal FAERS submissions, did exactly this:

| Year | Vioxx share of all MI reports in FAERS | Masking alert |
|------|----------------------------------------|---------------|
| 2004 | **33%** | ✓ |
| 2005 | **68%** | ✓ |
| 2006 | **72%** | ✓ (worst year) |
| 2007 | 15% (Vioxx withdrawn by now; Celebrex/Bextra dominate) | ✓ |

---

**Lead times:**
- Masking-corrected screen **led the Nissen meta-analysis by ~1 year** and the FDA boxed warning by ~23 months.
- Standard screen **lagged the Nissen meta-analysis by ~7 months** and the FDA warning by ~1 month. In other words, FAERS told regulators nothing they didn't already know from published clinical science.

---

### 4. Why the Standard Screen Looked the Way It Did

In 2006 the standard cumulative PRR was **0.76** — *below 1.0*, implying apparent "protection" against MI. This is a statistical artifact, not biology. With 72% of all MI reports in FAERS belonging to Vioxx that year, the background MI rate was ~5.5%. Rosiglitazone's actual MI rate (~3.75%) looked low by comparison. The drug appeared *protective* — the exact opposite of reality.

The 2007 annual-only PRR (year-level, not cumulative) *did* cross the threshold (PRR 2.30, χ² 137), but the cumulative series — the one used to make formal regulatory calls — remained below threshold until 2008, because years of Vioxx-diluted data dragged the average down.

---

### 5. Implications for How Signal Detection Should Be Run

**i. Routine masking detection should be mandatory.** The `top_contributor` column is not optional metadata — it is a validity check on the PRR itself. Whenever a single drug holds ≥25% of a reaction's background reports, the PRR for every *other* drug with that reaction is potentially suppressed. The corrected analysis should run automatically in parallel with the standard one.

**ii. PRR below 1.0 should trigger investigation, not reassurance.** A drug appearing "protective" against a serious adverse event is almost always a sign that a high-reporting confounder has inflated the denominator. The 2005–2006 rosiglitazone result (PRR 0.76–0.80 for MI) should have been treated as an analytical anomaly requiring explanation, not as evidence of safety.

**iii. Litigation-driven reporting spikes distort the entire database.** When a drug is sued at scale, plaintiff attorneys and healthcare providers submit thousands of FAERS reports in a short window. These are concentrated in one reaction category (here: MI for Vioxx), creating a temporary but severe distortion of the background for *all other drugs* sharing that reaction. FAERS mining systems should flag years where a single drug contributes an unusual share of any high-volume reaction.

**iv. Cumulative vs. annual PRR serve different functions.** Annual PRR caught a signal in 2007 (the year Vioxx noise finally cleared). Cumulative PRR did not catch it until 2008, because the accumulated suppression from prior years pulled the statistic down. In a real-time surveillance system, *both* series must be monitored; a sudden annual signal that the cumulative series has not yet confirmed still warrants a triage review.

**v. FAERS spontaneous reporting is a lagging, not a leading, indicator — unless it is actively de-confounded.** Without masking correction, FAERS for rosiglitazone confirmed what clinicians already published. With masking correction, it would have preceded clinical evidence by a year. The difference is not in the data — the same reports existed — it is entirely in the analytical method. This is a process failure, not a data failure.

### Likely Reporting Biases

**1. Litigation-driven notoriety bias (dominant distortion).** Vioxx was withdrawn in September 2004 following the APPROVe trial. The subsequent mass-tort litigation — one of the largest in US pharmaceutical history — generated a wave of plaintiff-initiated and attorney-facilitated FAERS submissions concentrated on cardiovascular outcomes. The effect is visible in the data: reports received in 2014 (a decade after withdrawal) still cite Vioxx as a suspect drug. PRR values in the tens to hundreds of thousands of χ² reflect this reporting avalanche, not real-world incidence at those magnitudes. Critically, as demonstrated in the rosiglitazone analysis, these same reports *inflated the background MI rate for every other drug in FAERS* during the 2004–2007 period.

**2. Co-reporting of Avandia.** Multiple retrieved cases co-report AVANDIA (rosiglitazone), itself the subject of independent cardiovascular litigation and FAERS submissions. This co-suspect overlap means some Vioxx-labelled cases are actually cases where the reporter considered both drugs implicated; the two drugs' signals are not independent.

**3. Channelling and indication confounding.** Rofecoxib was prescribed preferentially to patients with arthritic conditions, who are inherently at elevated cardiovascular risk. Spontaneous reports cannot adjust for this baseline risk differential.

**4. Suppression of non-cardiovascular reactions.** The flood of CV-coded reports proportionally reduces the apparent signal for GI and musculoskeletal reactions — the very endpoints rofecoxib was licensed to treat — making musculoskeletal PRRs appear modest despite clinical plausibility.

**5. Under-reporting of the comparator.** PRR compares rofecoxib report rates against all other drugs in FAERS. Drugs without litigation history are under-reported for serious events, artificially widening the ratio.

---

### Recommended Next Step

The disproportionality signal for rofecoxib × cardiovascular outcomes is unambiguous and biologically coherent, but the PRR magnitudes are not usable as-is for quantitative risk estimation. Three actions are warranted:

1. **Litigation-period stratification:** Re-run the PRR series restricted to reports received *before* the September 2004 withdrawal and *before* the first major litigation settlement wave (~2005–2007). Pre-withdrawal PRRs are less confounded and would give a cleaner picture of what the spontaneous-report database actually said during active marketing.

2. **Masking impact audit:** Using the same technique applied to the rosiglitazone analysis, quantify how much Vioxx's dominance of the MI and CVA background (32–72% in 2004–2006) suppressed detectable signals for co-prescribed drugs in the same patient population.

3. **Cross-reference with controlled evidence:** The VIGOR trial (2000), APPROVe trial (2004), and Bresalier et al. (NEJM 2005) established relative risk estimates under controlled conditions. Those estimates — not the FAERS PRRs — should anchor any quantitative risk communication. The FAERS analysis confirms signal direction and biological plausibility; it cannot substitute for trial-derived effect sizes.
